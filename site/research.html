Research questions

The five questions every programme answers

01

Efficiency

Does the material improve nitrogen and phosphorus use efficiency in these soils, and by how much?

02

Substitution

Can fertilizer rates be reduced while holding yield — and at what application rate of the material?

03

Soil function

What happens to aggregate stability, water-holding capacity and exchange capacity over one to three seasons?

04

Water

Does nutrient leaching or runoff change? This is where agronomy meets watershed and regulatory interest.

05

Economics

What is the return per acre at current fertilizer and freight prices, including the delivered cost of the material? A material that works agronomically but not economically is reported as exactly that.

Study standards

Design rules we do not bend

These are the conditions that separate a trial from a testimonial.

Trial design

  • Randomized and replicated, never a single strip
  • An untreated control in every trial
  • At least three arms: control, label rate, label rate with reduced fertilizer
  • Treatments assigned before the season, not chosen after
  • Pre-registered endpoints, so the measure cannot move to fit the result

Measurement

  • Baseline soil sampling before treatment, same points after
  • Independent university or core-facility laboratories
  • Tissue analysis for uptake, not yield alone
  • Statistical analysis stated with the trial, including the power we have
  • Raw data retained and available to funders and reviewers

The variable matrix

One material, many conditions

A soil amendment rarely performs the same way twice. The programme is built to find the combinations where the response is real and repeatable — starting with natural pozzolan.

Crop

Corn and soybean first, then a forage or specialty crop, each with its own nutrient demand pattern.

Soil component

Texture, clay content, organic matter, pH and baseline exchange capacity: silt loam, claypan and bottomland.

Rate & blend

Application rate, blend ratio with conventional fertilizer, and placement — broadcast, banded or incorporated.

Season & climate

Rainfall, temperature and drought stress across more than one growing year, because a single season proves little.

Questions

Frequently asked

Will you publish a result that makes a product look bad?

Yes. The right to publish all results, positive or negative, is written into our governing documents and is not subject to supplier approval. It is the reason the data is worth anything.

You were founded by executives of a pozzolan producer. How is that independent?

It is disclosed, not hidden — in every application, study and report. Independence comes from structure: neutral laboratories, university and core-facility scientists as principal investigators, pre-registered endpoints, and an unconditional right to publish.

How long before there is data?

Controlled lab and greenhouse work can produce early signal within a season. Replicated field results worth citing take two growing years, and grant timelines usually set the pace.

Do you make yield claims?

Not ahead of the data. We report fertilizer-efficiency and soil-property outcomes from our own replicated trials, and we label anything drawn from existing literature as literature.

Can we fund a study on our own material?

Yes, and we will tell you before you commit what the study can and cannot prove. We also look for grant programmes that can carry part of the cost.

What we measure

Three questions decide whether an input is worth using

Growers do not buy chemistry, they buy outcomes. Every study answers the same three questions in numbers an agronomist and a farm manager can both act on.

Nutrient-use efficiency

How much applied nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium reaches the crop instead of the air or the water — and whether rates can be cut while holding yield.

Soil function

Cation exchange capacity, water-holding capacity, aggregate stability and pH response, measured in the soils the crop is actually grown in.

Farm economics

Delivered cost per acre, the fertilizer dollars displaced, and the return at current prices. A result that does not pay is reported as such.

How we work

Evidence is built in stages, and each stage earns the next

We start at the cheapest level that can disprove the hypothesis. Nothing moves to field scale until the controlled work says it should.

STAGE 01

Characterize

Mineralogy, particle size, exchange capacity and nutrient retention when blended with representative soils.

STAGE 02

Controlled growth

Greenhouse rate trials crossing application rate against full and reduced fertilizer, with uptake and leachate measured.

STAGE 03

Replicated field

Randomized on-farm plots with cooperating growers: untreated control, label rate, and label rate with reduced fertilizer.

STAGE 04

Analyze & publish

Statistical analysis, an economic model per acre, an open technical report, and data that supports accurate labeling.

Claims standard. We report fertilizer-efficiency and soil-property outcomes only. We make no pest or disease claims. Any figure drawn from existing literature is labelled as literature, not as a result of our trials, until our own replicated data supports it.
The analysis layer

From a lab result to a rate someone can actually use

A number on a lab report is not a recommendation. We model trial data across rate, crop, soil component and season, so the output is the rate and blend where response peaks — for a named crop in a named soil, with the economics attached.

  • Response curves, not single data points
  • Crop, soil texture, blend ratio, placement and weather carried as explicit variables
  • Stated confidence, including where the data is thin
  • Delivered cost, fertilizer displaced and net return per acre
First material

Natural pozzolan, tested across crops and conditions

Pozzolan is the first material through our pipeline: a porous, silica-rich volcanic mineral with a long record in construction and a thin one in Midwest agronomy. The programme is built to find where it helps, by how much, and where it does not.

  • Corn and soybean first, then a forage or specialty crop
  • Missouri soils: silt loam, claypan and river bottomland
  • Several application rates, crossed with full and reduced fertilizer
  • Blended with conventional fertilizer, not only applied alone
  • More than one growing season, so weather is a variable not an excuse

Independence by design

Research anyone can check

An input company can fund research. It cannot credibly grade its own homework. Our structure is built so a sceptical agronomist, grant reviewer or regulator can follow the work.

Neutral laboratories

Soil, tissue and water analysis runs through independent university and core facilities, not an in-house bench.

Publish either way

Positive, negative or inconclusive, the result is published. No supplier holds approval over what we report.

Named scientists

University and research-institute scientists serve as principal investigators and advisors on the studies they lead.

What we measure

Three questions decide whether an input is worth using

Growers do not buy chemistry, they buy outcomes. Every study we design answers the same three questions in numbers a farm manager and an agronomist can both act on.

Nutrient-use efficiency

How much of the applied nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium reaches the crop instead of the air or the water — and whether rates can be reduced while holding yield.

Soil function

Cation exchange capacity, water-holding capacity, aggregate stability and pH response, measured in the soil types the crop is actually grown in.

Farm economics

Cost per acre delivered, the fertilizer dollars displaced, and the return at current input prices. A result that does not pay is reported as such.

How we work

Evidence is built in stages, and each stage earns the next

We start at the cheapest level that can disprove the hypothesis. Nothing moves to field scale until the controlled work says it should.

Stage 01

Characterize

Mineralogy, particle size, exchange capacity and nutrient retention when blended with representative soils.

Stage 02

Controlled growth

Greenhouse rate trials crossing application rate against full and reduced fertilizer, with uptake and leachate measured.

Stage 03

Replicated field

Randomized on-farm strip and plot trials with cooperating growers. Untreated control, label rate, and label rate with reduced fertilizer.

Stage 04

Analyze & publish

Statistical analysis, an economic model per acre, an open technical report, and data that supports accurate labeling.

Claims standard. We report fertilizer-efficiency and soil-property outcomes only. We make no pest or disease claims. Any figure drawn from existing literature is labelled as literature, not as a result of our trials, until our own replicated data supports it.
The analysis layer

From a lab result to an application rate someone can use

A number on a lab report is not a recommendation. We model the trial data against crop, soil component, rate, blend and season so the output is a rate and a blend for a named situation — with the economics attached.

Modelled, not guessed

Trial results are fitted across rate, crop and soil type so the response curve, not a single data point, drives the recommendation.

Variable by variable

Crop, soil texture and chemistry, blend ratio, application rate, placement, season and weather are carried as explicit variables.

A rate, not a range

The deliverable is the rate and blend that maximised response for that crop and soil, with the confidence we actually have.

Priced per acre

Every recommendation carries delivered cost, displaced fertilizer and net return, so a grower can compare it to doing nothing.

First material

Natural pozzolan, tested across crops and conditions

Pozzolan is the first material through our pipeline. It is a porous, silica-rich volcanic mineral with a long record in construction and a thin one in Midwest agronomy. Our programme is built to find where it helps, by how much, and where it does not.

What we are testing against

  • Row crops first — corn and soybean — then a forage or specialty crop
  • Missouri soil types: silt loam, claypan and river bottomland
  • Multiple application rates, crossed with full and reduced fertilizer
  • Blends with conventional fertilizer, not only standalone application
  • Season and weather variation across more than one growing year

What a result looks like

  • The rate at which response peaks, by crop and soil type
  • How far fertilizer can be cut while holding yield, if at all
  • Change in soil function over one to three seasons
  • Nutrient movement toward water, measured not assumed
  • Return per acre at current fertilizer and freight prices

Independence by design

Research anyone can check

An input company can fund research. It cannot credibly grade its own homework. Our structure is built so a sceptical agronomist, grant reviewer or regulator can follow the work.

Neutral laboratories

Soil, tissue and water analysis runs through independent university and core facilities, not an in-house bench.

Publish either way

Positive, negative or inconclusive, the result is published. No supplier holds approval over what we report.

Named scientists

University and research-institute scientists serve as principal investigators and advisors on the studies they lead.

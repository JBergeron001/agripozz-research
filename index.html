Agri-Pozz Field Research Initiative

A proposed St. Louis agricultural research venture led by Jay Bergeron and Han Ko. We will independently test Agri-Pozz, a natural volcanic pozzolan soil amendment, in Midwest soils and crops, using grant funding and the region's shared research infrastructure, and publish credible, peer-quality data.

Overview

Fertilizer is one of the largest input costs on Midwest farms, and nutrient loss is a leading water-quality problem in the Mississippi basin. Natural pozzolans are porous, silica-rich volcanic minerals. Published field-trial literature reports fertilizer-use reductions of up to 30% with mineral soil amendments of this class. Agri-Pozz now needs independent, replicated, Midwest-specific data before growers, agronomists and regulators can rely on it.

The product

Agri-Pozz

Natural volcanic pumicite from an Eastern California deposit, lab-certified to ASTM C618 Class N, about 68% SiO2. Produced by Environmental Minerals, Inc. (EMI).

The gap

No Midwest data yet

The fertilizer-reduction potential comes from published literature on this mineral class. EMI's own trials are scheduled. Midwest growers need local soils, local crops and neutral researchers.

The venture

Independent research company

A small St. Louis company that designs, funds and manages the studies, using non-dilutive grants and fee-for-service labs, and owns and publishes the resulting dataset.

Claims standard. All Agri-Pozz language on this page is about fertilizer efficiency and soil properties only. We make no pest or disease claims. "Up to 30%" refers to published field-trial literature; Agri-Pozz-specific trials are the purpose of this initiative.

Research plan

Staged from low-cost lab work to replicated field trials, so each grant funds the next level of evidence.

Stage 1 · Characterize

Material & soil-interaction baseline

Mineralogy, cation exchange capacity, water-holding capacity, pH effect and nutrient retention when blended with typical Missouri soils (silt loam, claypan, river bottomland).

Stage 2 · Controlled growth

Greenhouse rate trials

Corn, soybean and a forage or specialty crop at several application rates, crossed with full and reduced fertilizer rates. Measure biomass, nutrient uptake, water use and leachate.

Stage 3 · Field

Replicated on-farm plots

Randomized strip or plot trials with cooperating Missouri growers. Treatments include an untreated control, the label rate, and the label rate with reduced fertilizer. Yield, soil health indicators and economics.

Stage 4 · Share

Publish & extend

An open technical report, field days with extension partners, data for state regulatory filings, and a commercialization package that EMI and distributors can use.

Core research questions

  1. Does Agri-Pozz improve nitrogen and phosphorus use efficiency in Missouri soils, and by how much?
  2. Can yields be held while fertilizer rates are reduced, and at what Agri-Pozz rate?
  3. What happens to soil health indicators (aggregate stability, water-holding capacity, CEC) over one to three seasons?
  4. Does it reduce nutrient leaching or runoff? This links to water quality and to watershed-scale benefits.
  5. What is the grower's return per acre at current fertilizer prices?

Near-zero out-of-pocket model. Free legal (WashU clinic), free grant-writing help (SBDC FAST, BioGenerator), donated product (EMI), in-kind plots (growers, MFA) and grant-funded lab work cover most startup costs. The main remaining gap is timing: grants pay after award, so small early costs (filing fees, insurance, first lab tests) may need short-term bridge funding.
Regulatory path. Missouri regulates soil conditioners under the Soil Conditioners Law (RSMo 266.361–266.400). The Missouri Department of Agriculture states it does not register soil amendments. If any nutrient guarantee is made, Missouri Fertilizer Law applies, administered by the Missouri Fertilizer Control Board. Our trial data will support accurate labeling in Missouri and neighboring states, several of which (for example Kansas and Illinois) register soil amendments.

Process & timeline

What we have to do, in order. Dates are targets.

  1. Nov – Dec 2026
    Form the company and set the ground rules
    • Form the entity and sign a founders agreement (Jay Bergeron and Han Ko): roles, equity, decision rights.
    • Sign a research and material-supply agreement with EMI covering sample supply, data ownership, publication rights and independence of results, plus permission to use the Agri-Pozz name.
    • Register with SAM.gov (UEI), Grants.gov and SBIR.gov. Registration can take several weeks, so start immediately.
  2. Dec 2026 – Jan 2027
    Plug into the St. Louis ecosystem
    • Intake meeting with BioGenerator Labs (Helix Center) on lab access and the Grants to Business program.
    • Scoping call with Danforth Center Core Facilities to price a greenhouse and phenotyping study.
    • Identify an Extension, SWCD or certified-crop-advisor partner and two to four cooperating growers. If timing allows, submit an NCR-SARE proposal.
  3. Winter – Spring 2027
    Stage 1–2 research (lab and greenhouse)
    • Material characterization, soil blends and rate trials through fee-for-service cores and MU labs.
    • Apply to Arch Grants (spring window) using the early data.
  4. Spring – Fall 2027
    Stage 3 field season and SBIR/STTR Phase I
    • Replicated field plots with cooperating growers; soil health sampling before and after.
    • Prepare and submit the USDA SBIR/STTR Phase I application (fall cycle) with BioGenerator coaching.
  5. 2028
    Publish, scale, commercialize
    • Release a technical report and hold field days; feed data into state labeling and registrations.
    • Apply for SBIR Phase II and NRCS CIG On-Farm Trials for multi-state, multi-grower scale-up.
    • Hand a validated commercialization package to EMI and its distribution partners.

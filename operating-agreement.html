Operating Agreement of Bedrock Agronomics, LLC

Draft. For co-founder review. Not for signature. To be reviewed by the WashU Law Entrepreneurship Clinic or a Missouri attorney before signing. Not legal advice. Bracketed items [like this] are open choices to fill in together.

This Operating Agreement is made effective [date] by Han Ko and Jeremy ("Jay") Bergeron (each a "Member") under the Missouri Limited Liability Company Act, RSMo Chapter 347 (the "Act").

Article 1 — Formation

1.1 Name. Bedrock Agronomics, LLC (the "Company").

1.2 Formation. The Company was formed by filing Articles of Organization with the Missouri Secretary of State on [date].

1.3 Office and agent. Principal office: [address]. Registered agent: [name and Missouri address].

1.4 Term. Perpetual, until dissolved under Article 13.

Article 2 — Purpose

2.1 The Company conducts independent agricultural and soil research, starting with natural pozzolan soil amendments (including Agri-Pozz), and any lawful related activity.

2.2 The Company will seek grants, research contracts and in-kind support, and may publish and license its research results.

Article 3 — Members and Units

3.1 Units. Ownership is measured in Units. 10,000 Units are issued:

3.2 Voting. Members vote by percentage, except Major Decisions (Article 6), which need both Members' written consent while there are only two Members.

3.3 Vesting. Each Member's Units vest over [4] years from [date]: [25%] after one year, the rest in equal monthly amounts. Unvested Units may be repurchased at the lower of cost or fair value if a Member leaves. All Units vest on a sale of the Company.

3.4 New Members. Admitted only by Major Decision and on signing this Agreement.

3.5 Limited liability. No Member is personally liable for Company debts beyond its capital contribution, except as the Act requires.

Article 4 — Capital

4.1 Initial contributions. Each Member contributes $[100] cash on signing.

4.2 No further contributions are required. Additional capital or Member loans need a Major Decision.

4.3 No interest is paid on capital, and no Member may withdraw capital except as this Agreement provides.

Article 5 — Management and Officers

5.1 Manager. The Company is manager-managed. Han Ko is the Manager and runs the business, subject to Article 6.

5.2 Officers.

OfficerHolderMain duties
Chief Executive OfficerHan KoStrategy, capital, partnerships, finance, senior relationships, contracts
President and Director of Research ProgramsJay BergeronDay-to-day operations, research program, study design, grants, lab and grower partners; second in command

5.3 Second in command. The President acts with the CEO's authority when the CEO is unavailable, or conflicted on a matter, for up to [30] days or until the CEO returns.

5.4 Signing authority. CEO: contracts and financial documents. President: research protocols, grant narratives, partner letters. Either: routine items under $[5,000].

5.5 Removal. The Manager or an officer may be replaced only by Major Decision, or for Cause (fraud, gross negligence, willful misconduct, or material breach not cured within 30 days of notice).

5.6 Duties. The Manager and officers act in good faith and in the Company's best interest, subject to Article 7.

5.7 Compensation. None unless budgeted in an awarded grant or approved by Major Decision. Reasonable documented expenses are reimbursed.

Article 6 — Major Decisions

6.1 While the Company has two Members, these need the written consent of both:

  1. Admitting a Member or issuing Units, options or convertible securities.
  2. Borrowing, guaranteeing or spending over $[25,000] in one transaction or series.
  3. Any transaction with EMI or any Member's affiliate (Article 7).
  4. Selling, merging, converting or dissolving the Company, or selling most of its assets.
  5. Amending this Agreement or the Articles of Organization.
  6. Taking outside investment or applying to Missouri Technology Corporation programs.
  7. Changing the Company's purpose.
  8. Setting or changing any Member's or officer's pay.
  9. Replacing the Manager or an officer, except for Cause.
  10. Starting or settling litigation over $[10,000].

6.2 Consent may be by email or e-signature.

Article 7 — Conflicts of Interest and EMI

7.1 Disclosure. Each Member discloses that he is an executive of Environmental Minerals, Inc. ("EMI"), and Jay Bergeron is a part owner of EMI and the applicant for the AGRI-POZZ trademark. Each Member will disclose any new conflict promptly in writing.

7.2 EMI transactions. Any agreement with EMI (product supply, data sharing, licensing, services) must be:

  • in writing;
  • on terms no less favorable to the Company than an arm's-length deal;
  • approved by both Members; and
  • disclosed to EMI's board and to any grant funder that requires conflict disclosure.

7.3 Independent check. For any EMI agreement over $[10,000] in value, the Members will get a short written fairness review from an independent advisor (for example the Company's attorney or a grant program advisor).

7.4 Outside activities. Members may work for EMI and other businesses. EMI's business (mining, processing and selling pozzolan and related products) is not a Company opportunity. Research services for third parties on soil amendments are Company opportunities and are offered to the Company first.

7.5 No EMI control. EMI has no right to approve, delay or edit Company research, results or publications.

Article 8 — Intellectual Property and Research Independence

8.1 Company IP. Data, protocols, reports, software and inventions created by or for the Company belong to the Company. Each Member assigns such work to the Company.

8.2 Pre-existing IP. Each Member and EMI keep IP they owned before this Agreement. The Company uses the Agri-Pozz name and product only under a written license or supply agreement (Article 7).

8.3 Publication. The Company may publish all results, positive or negative. Grant terms on publication control where they apply.

8.4 Confidentiality. Members keep Company confidential information private during membership and for [3] years after.

Article 9 — Transfers and Buyouts

9.1 No transfer of Units without the other Member's written consent, except to a trust for estate planning that agrees to this Agreement.

9.2 Right of first refusal. A Member who receives a good-faith offer must first offer the Units to the other Member on the same terms, open for [30] days.

9.3 Buyout events. On a Member's death, permanent disability, bankruptcy, or voluntary departure, the Company or the other Member may buy that Member's vested Units at Fair Value, paid over up to [36] months.

9.4 Fair Value is agreed by the Members, or else set by an independent appraiser chosen by both (cost shared equally).

9.5 Tag-along / drag-along. If a Member holding over 50% sells all its Units, the other Member may sell on the same terms (tag). On a sale of the whole Company approved under Article 6, both Members sell on the same terms (drag).

Article 10 — Deadlock

10.1 If the Members cannot agree on a Major Decision after [30] days of good-faith discussion, they will mediate in St. Louis County, Missouri, sharing the cost.

10.2 If mediation fails within [60] days, either Member may start a buy-sell: name a price for all Units; the other Member must, within [45] days, either sell its Units or buy the offering Member's Units at that price.

Article 11 — Allocations and Distributions

11.1 Allocations. Profits and losses are allocated by percentage (51% / 49%), subject to tax rules on capital accounts.

11.2 Distributions. Made when the Manager decides, by percentage, after reserves for grant obligations and operations.

11.3 Tax distributions. If the Company has taxable income, it will try to distribute enough cash for Members to pay the related tax.

11.4 Grant funds are spent only as each grant allows and are never distributed to Members.

Article 12 — Books, Records and Tax

12.1 Tax status. The Company is taxed as a partnership unless the Members choose otherwise by Major Decision.

12.2 Books. Kept on a calendar-year basis at the principal office, with grant funds tracked separately. Each Member may inspect them on reasonable notice.

12.3 Partnership representative. Han Ko, for federal tax purposes.

12.4 Bank accounts. In the Company's name only. Payments over $[5,000] need approval of both officers.

Article 13 — Dissolution

13.1 The Company dissolves on a Major Decision to dissolve, a court order, or as the Act requires.

13.2 On dissolution, the Manager winds up the business, completes or transfers grant obligations, pays creditors, then distributes remaining assets by positive capital account balances.

13.3 Research data and reports remain publicly available where already published.

Article 14 — General Provisions

14.1 Indemnification. The Company indemnifies Members, the Manager and officers for good-faith acts within their authority, except fraud, gross negligence or willful misconduct.

14.2 Insurance. The Company will carry general liability insurance before field work, and directors-and-officers insurance when funds allow.

14.3 Notices go by email to the addresses on Schedule A, with copy by mail for notices of default or buyout.

14.4 Governing law and venue. Missouri law; courts in St. Louis County, Missouri.

14.5 Entire agreement. This Agreement replaces the term sheet and prior understandings.

14.6 Severability and counterparts. An invalid term does not affect the rest. E-signatures and counterparts are valid.
